# Patent intelligence vs Google Patents: a working guide

If you're new to patent research, you've probably used Google Patents. It's free, fast, and indexes over 120 million patent publications from 17 patent offices. For finding a specific patent number or doing a keyword search, it's the starting point.

But patent intelligence platforms serve a different purpose. Here's when to use each.

## Google Patents: best for search and discovery

**Strengths:**
- **Full-text search** across 120M+ patents from USPTO, EPO, WIPO, JPO, KIPO, and others
- **Prior art finder** — Google's "Similar" button uses embeddings to surface related prior art
- **Classification browsing** — CPC and IPC taxonomies with human-readable descriptions
- **Free, no account required**

**Limitations:**
- **No signal detection.** You see what you search for. You don't get alerts when something changes.
- **No assignee intelligence.** You can filter by assignee, but you can't see filing velocity, topic shifts, or strategic analysis.
- **No expiry tracking.** Google Patents shows legal status events but doesn't flag expiring patents by opportunity score.
- **No trend analysis.** You can't ask "what CPC areas are accelerating this month?"

Google Patents is a library. It's excellent for finding a specific book. It doesn't tell you which books are being read the most, which authors are publishing in new genres, or which books are about to enter the public domain.

## Invention Index 8: best for signal detection

**Strengths:**
- **Daily signal refresh** — filing trends, citation velocity, expiry windows update every day
- **Opportunity scoring** — composite score per patent that weights all eight signals
- **Assignee profiling** — track what companies are filing, in what areas, at what velocity
- **Automated alerts** — webhook-based notifications when a followed company files in your watched areas
- **Briefing emails** — weekly editorial summary of what changed in your topics

**Limitations:**
- Not a replacement for full-text search (use Google Patents for that)
- Focused on active monitoring, not archival research
- Currently USPTO/EPO/WIPO coverage (expanding to JPO/KIPO in Phase 4)

## The recommended workflow

Here's how we use both tools at Invention Index 8:

### Step 1: Monitor with II8
Set up your topics (themes + companies). Check the daily briefing. Set alerts for companies you're tracking competitively. Let the signals tell you *what changed*.

### Step 2: Deep-dive with Google Patents
When II8 surfaces a signal — say, a trend spike in G06N or a high-opportunity expiring patent — take the patent number to Google Patents for full-text reading, prior art exploration, and classification browsing.

### Step 3: Verify with USPTO Public PAIR
For legal status confirmation (especially for patents you're considering licensing or challenging), the USPTO's Public PAIR system is the authoritative source. Neither Google Patents nor II8 replaces official legal research.

### Step 4: Track outcomes
As you make decisions based on patent intelligence — investment, product development, competitive positioning — log the signal that triggered the action. Over time, you'll learn which signals have the highest predictive value for your domain.

## The bottom line

Google Patents is a search engine for patents. Invention Index 8 is a signal detection platform. They're complementary tools in a patent researcher's workflow, not competitors.

Use Google Patents to find. Use II8 to know what to look for.
