# How to read a patent without a law degree

Patents are dense. Legal jargon. Run-on sentences that span half a page. Cross-references to other documents you'll never read. It's no wonder most people give up after the title.

But here's the thing: **90% of a patent's value lives in sections you can scan in under two minutes.** You don't need to parse the claims. You don't need Black's Law Dictionary. You need a framework.

## The 2-minute patent scan

Open any patent. Skip the abstract (it's usually boilerplate). Go straight to:

### 1. The title (5 seconds)

Does the problem space interest you? Move on or bail here. Life's too short for patents you don't care about.

### 2. The first figure + caption (15 seconds)

Inventors are visual. The first figure usually shows the high-level architecture or process flow. If Figure 1 doesn't explain what this thing does, the patent is badly written.

### 3. The "Field of the Invention" paragraph (20 seconds)

Usually the first substantive paragraph after the background section. It tells you:
- What problem this patent solves
- What technical domain it operates in

Example: "The present invention relates to a method for training neural networks using federated learning across distributed edge devices."

That tells you: AI/ML, federated, edge computing. If you know these terms, you're qualified to understand this patent.

### 4. The "Summary of the Invention" section (30 seconds)

This is the inventor's elevator pitch. It often repeats the independent claims in plain English. Look for sentences that start with "The invention provides..." or "Embodiments include..."

### 5. Check the assignee and filing date (10 seconds)

Who owns this? When did they file? This tells you:
- Whether this is a defensive filing by a giant (Google, Samsung) or an offensive move by a startup
- Whether there's an expiry window worth tracking (20 years from filing)
- Whether this company has been working in this space for a while (check their other filings)

## What to SKIP

Don't bother with the claims on a first pass. Seriously. Claims are for patent examiners and litigators. They're written in a specialized grammar ("a method comprising...", "wherein said...") designed to define legal boundaries, not communicate ideas.

Also skip:
- The detailed description (unless you're building something)
- The "Background of the Invention" (it's a literature review, often padded)
- The abstract (it's a legal formality)

## The numbers you want

When we analyze a patent on Invention Index 8, we look at:

- **CPC codes**: What technical area does this fall into? (G06N = AI/ML, H01L = semiconductors)
- **Citation count**: How many later patents reference this one? High counts = foundational
- **Assignee activity**: Is this company ramping up filings in this area? (trend signal)
- **Expiry date**: When does this patent expire? Expiring patents create white space for competitors

## Try it yourself

Pick any patent from our database. Scan the title, Figure 1, and Field of Invention. You'll be surprised how much you understand in 2 minutes.

The real skill isn't reading patents — it's knowing which ones to read.
